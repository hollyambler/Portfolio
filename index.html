Case Study · 2026

Cross-Product Journey Investigation

Context

Our division was reorganized, bringing Intune, Microsoft's enterprise endpoint management solution, and Purview, its data governance and compliance solution, under the auspices of a single team. The CVP of the new Management and Data Security org needed to know about any cross-product moments between the two products and how the two different types of admins who use them might or might not interact, both within and outside of the Microsoft admin portals.

Goal

Identify top cross-product workflows, capture task handoff mechanisms, document pain points, and identify areas of opportunity.

Approach

AI-assisted literature review; cross-portal telemetry data analysis; Support ticket deep dive, rapid JTBD research with Intune and Purview admins to validate workflows and job steps; survey to capture top tasks, major pain points, and opportunities to improve at scale. Recommendations delivered through an easily interpreted "Frequency vs. Pain" matrix, with a "Read this if you only have 2 minutes" slide communicating major findings.

Key Insights
  • Cross-product journeys are rare.
  • At enterprises, the two types of admins (IT Pros for Intune, Compliance and Data Security professionals for Purview) seldom access their counterparts' portals in accordance with principles of Least Privilege.
  • Security professionals define the posture, and IT Pros figure out how to implement it. Communication and task management occur outside of Microsoft tooling.
  • Silent configurations and ambiguity about permissions are the top pain point for both segments. Cross-product notifications to combat silent configurations and clearer guidance about which team should own protecting corporate data on BYOD devices are the top opportunities.
Impact

This research laid the foundation for ongoing Secure Enterprise Browser improvements and the next phase of Intune-Purview interoperability.

My role

Research lead: The Partner Product Design Director reached out to me to uncover all I could, as fast as I could, about customer journeys that spanned both Intune and Purview. I drafted and drove the research plan, tapped my research counterpart on the Purview team to partner with me, created and administered the project workspace, led the literature review, recruited participants, ran JTBD research, and created a rapid qualitative share-out of my findings using Copilot. In parallel, I worked with PM partners to pull telemetry data and with Support partners to capture ticket volume and themes. My research partner and I collaborated on a survey. We also collaborated on the final report, which I delivered to key stakeholders.

What this showsProject leadership, research rigor, AI adoption, stakeholder management, bias for action, storytelling skills.

Methods and ToolsJTBD, Survey, Top Tasks Analysis. Copilot Cowork for lit review, analysis and first-draft report generation, Microsoft Loop to coordinate the team and rapidly socialize "Research Nuggets."

Case Study · 2026

Platform Pivot Survey

Context

A new principal PM noticed that Intune users sometimes pivot to the other Security suite portals mid-task. The Partner Product Design Director asked me to discover why.

Goal

Discover when and why Intune users drop out of the task funnel; complement my PM partner's parallel telemetry investigation; validate previous qualitative research about user pivots.

Approach

Lit review of previous research; stakeholder interviews to understand problem space and hypotheses; survey recruited through the Intune Customer Connection Panel, which is a Microsoft-maintained user community.

Key Insights
  • Pivoting occurs only in a narrow set of scenarios and is driven by feature gaps.
  • The journey is round trip: Users do not abandon Intune but instead gather necessary information from the other portals, then return to complete their original task.
  • We can shorten journey depth by bringing Licensing information and Edge configuration capabilities into Intune.
Impact

Saved rework cycles, captured user motivations, uncovered product opportunities.

My role

Research lead: I ran a survey to uncover the scenarios where users leave Intune and their reasons for doing so and correlated those findings with their roles, company size, and license tier. I then worked with my PM partner to compare the survey findings to his telemetry data, which focused on which page they were on when they left and how often that pivot occurred. I crafted an easily digested report and delivered it to Executive Leadership.

What this showsProject leadership, research rigor, AI adoption, stakeholder management, bias for action, flexibility, storytelling skills.

Methods and ToolsSurvey, Microsoft Forms, Copilot Cowork for analysis and report acceleration.

Case Study · 2025

Copilot Entry Point Study

Context

This usability study compared two competing design systems to assess which one served IT Pros best, and why. The interview component of the study further explored sentiment around billing, and if and how user role affected Copilot adoption and usage.

Goal

Discover which entry point users select first and why; understand how their use cases and roles influenced that choice; assess how to optimize UI discoverability without straying into cognitive overload and information architecture polyhierarchy.

Approach

Within-participant usability study. 11 participants. High-fidelity prototype. Alternating task order to counteract primacy bias.

Key Insights
  • Multiple entry points confuse users, increase frustration, and slow task completion.
  • Users expect Copilot entry points to be contextually aware of their task, role, and usage patterns.
  • Relatedly, they expect a minimum number of turns to complete their tasks. To support this goal, provide suggested prompts to minimize "garbage in/garbage out" and ineffective prompting.
  • Large enterprises typically employ a chargeback model, making consumption harder to track. When they lack visibility about consumption, users limit Copilot usage.
  • If prompts are limited, they are typically allocated to SecOps instead of IT Pros. This factor constrained AI adoption for Intune teams at large enterprises.
  • Copilot was viewed as more valuable for junior admins than experienced admins or Solutions Architects.
Impact

Directly influenced the product and design direction and contributed important insights about license allocation patterns, which contributed to a shift in the licensing model.

My role

Research lead: Crafted the research and test plan. Organized and led the data wallow. Crafted the report, using plain language and actionable recommendations. Shared findings in major forums. Study was cited by other researchers.

What this showsResearch rigor, understanding of "questions behind the questions," thought leadership, business mindset.

Methods and ToolsUsability study, Figma.

Case Study · 2024

Agentic Workflows in Intune

Context

Agentic workflows represented the next leap forward for Intune's AI journey. A complicating factor, however, is the fact that Intune is the control plane for the IT production environment, and uptime is non-negotiable. How much control would Intune Admins be willing to cede to AI agents? What guardrails would need to be in place to support adoption? What kind of agentic experiences would Intune Admins want and need (and be willing to pay for)?

Goal

Learn what Intune admins expect from agentic experiences, including specifics about configuration, deployment, and monitoring workflows, permissions, audit requirements, and trust.

Approach

Generative research: 19 interviews with Intune admins and Solutions Architects. Rating exercise using the Cloud Security Alliance Autonomy Level Taxonomy.

Key Insights
  • Risk is the deciding factor: Agents cannot perform any tasks with a wide "blast radius" or those that could potentially result in data loss or data or device unavailability.
  • Admins cannot adopt agentic workflows until they've been successfully piloted, as in any other technology rollout.
  • Agents cannot use the permissions of human "pilots." They must instead use service accounts or their own credentials to comply with audit requirements and principles of zero trust.
  • Human must retain control over scope: Agents must explicitly obtain permission from the "human in the loop" to access certain resources or to perform certain actions, and those permissions must be re-granted periodically.
  • In some cases, deterministic automations are more appropriate than agentic workflows.
  • Agents must abide by the same change control, governance, and ITIL processes as human beings.
Impact

Foundational research that helped set the direction for agentic experiences. Article referenced by the entire design team and the Agents core PM group. Work cited by fellow researchers.
