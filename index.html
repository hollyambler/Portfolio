Case Study · Intune × Purview · 2026

Cross-Product Journey Investigation

When a reorg put Microsoft's enterprise endpoint-management and data-governance products under one team, leadership needed to know where — and whether — their two very different admins actually meet.

My role

Research lead — drove plan, partnered across research, PM & Support

Methods

AI-assisted lit review, telemetry, support tickets, JTBD, survey

Delivered to

CVP of the new Management & Data Security org

Context

Two products, two admins, one new org

Our division was reorganized, bringing Intune (Microsoft's enterprise endpoint-management solution) and Purview (its data governance and compliance solution) under a single team. The CVP of the new Management and Data Security org needed to know about any cross-product moments between the two products — and how the two different types of admin who use them might or might not interact, both within and outside the Microsoft admin portals.

Goal

Identify top cross-product workflows, capture task handoff mechanisms, document pain points, and identify areas of opportunity.

Approach

Move fast, triangulate hard

AI-assisted literature review; cross-portal telemetry data analysis; a Support ticket deep dive; rapid JTBD research with Intune and Purview admins to validate workflows and job steps; and a survey to capture top tasks, major pain points, and opportunities to improve at scale. Recommendations were delivered through an easily interpreted "Frequency vs. Pain" matrix, with a "Read this if you only have 2 minutes" slide communicating the major findings.

Key insights
  • Cross-product journeys are rare.
  • At enterprises, the two admin types — IT Pros for Intune, Compliance and Data Security professionals for Purview — seldom access their counterparts' portals, in accordance with the principle of Least Privilege.
  • Security professionals define the posture; IT Pros figure out how to implement it. Communication and task management happen outside Microsoft tooling.
  • Silent configurations and ambiguity about permissions are the top pain point for both segments. Cross-product notifications and clearer guidance about who owns protecting corporate data on BYOD devices are the top opportunities.
Impact

This research laid the foundation for ongoing Secure Enterprise Browser improvements and the next phase of Intune–Purview interoperability.

My role. The Partner Product Design Director asked me to uncover all I could, as fast as I could, about journeys spanning both products. I drafted and drove the research plan, tapped my Purview research counterpart to partner with me, stood up the project workspace, led the literature review, recruited participants, ran JTBD research, and built a rapid qualitative share-out using Copilot. In parallel I worked with PM partners to pull telemetry and with Support partners to capture ticket volume and themes. My research partner and I collaborated on the survey and the final report, which I delivered to key stakeholders.

Case Study · Microsoft Intune · 2026

Platform Pivot Survey

A new principal PM noticed Intune users sometimes pivot to other Security suite portals mid-task. I was asked to discover why — and found the journey was a round trip, not an exit.

My role

Research lead — survey design, analysis, exec delivery

Methods

Lit review, stakeholder interviews, survey

Delivered to

Executive leadership

Context

Why do users leave mid-task?

A new principal PM noticed that Intune users sometimes pivot to the other Security suite portals mid-task. The Partner Product Design Director asked me to discover why.

Goal

Discover when and why Intune users drop out of the task funnel; complement my PM partner's parallel telemetry investigation; and validate previous qualitative research about user pivots.

Approach

Pair the survey with telemetry

A lit review of previous research; stakeholder interviews to understand the problem space and hypotheses; and a survey recruited through the Intune Customer Connection Panel, a Microsoft-maintained user community.

Key insights
  • Pivoting occurs only in a narrow set of scenarios, and is driven by feature gaps.
  • The journey is a round trip: users don't abandon Intune — they gather what they need from other portals, then return to complete their original task.
  • Journey depth can be shortened by bringing Licensing information and Edge configuration capabilities into Intune.
Impact

Saved rework cycles, captured user motivations, and uncovered concrete product opportunities.

My role. I ran a survey to uncover the scenarios where users leave Intune and their reasons for doing so, then correlated those findings with role, company size, and license tier. I worked with my PM partner to compare the survey results against his telemetry — which page users were on when they left, and how often each pivot occurred — and crafted an easily digested report I delivered to executive leadership.

Case Study · Microsoft Copilot · 2025

Copilot Entry Point Study

A head-to-head usability study of two competing design systems — which entry point serves IT Pros best, and why — that also surfaced how licensing quietly shapes AI adoption.

My role

Research lead — plan, data wallow, reporting

Methods

Within-participant usability study · high-fidelity Figma prototype

Participants

11, with alternating task order to counter primacy bias

Context

Two design systems, one IT Pro

This usability study compared two competing design systems to assess which one served IT Pros best, and why. The interview component further explored sentiment around billing, and whether and how a user's role affected Copilot adoption and usage.

Goal

Discover which entry point users select first and why; understand how their use cases and roles influenced that choice; and assess how to optimize UI discoverability without straying into cognitive overload or information-architecture polyhierarchy.

Approach

Within-participant, counterbalanced

A within-participant usability study with 11 participants, a high-fidelity prototype, and alternating task order to counteract primacy bias.

Key insights
  • Multiple entry points confuse users, increase frustration, and slow task completion.
  • Users expect Copilot entry points to be contextually aware of their task, role, and usage patterns.
  • They expect a minimum number of turns to complete a task — so provide suggested prompts to minimize "garbage in / garbage out" and ineffective prompting.
  • Large enterprises typically use a chargeback model, making consumption hard to track. When users lack visibility into consumption, they limit their Copilot usage.
  • When prompts are limited, they're typically allocated to SecOps rather than IT Pros — constraining AI adoption for Intune teams at large enterprises.
  • Copilot was seen as more valuable for junior admins than for experienced admins or Solutions Architects.
Impact

Directly influenced product and design direction, and contributed insights about license-allocation patterns that fed a shift in the licensing model.

My role. I crafted the research and test plan, organized and led the data wallow, and wrote the report in plain language with actionable recommendations. I shared findings in major forums, and the study was cited by other researchers.

Case Study · Microsoft Intune · 2024

Agentic Workflows in Intune

Intune is the control plane for the IT production environment, and uptime is non-negotiable. So how much control would admins actually cede to AI agents — and what guardrails would adoption require?

My role

Research lead — end to end

Methods

Generative research · cluster analysis

Participants

19 Intune admins & Solutions Architects

Context

The next leap — with uptime on the line

Agentic workflows represented the next leap forward for Intune's AI journey. The complicating factor: Intune is the control plane for the IT production environment, and uptime is non-negotiable. How much control would Intune admins be willing to cede to AI agents? What guardrails would need to be in place to support adoption? And what kind of agentic experiences would admins want, need — and be willing to pay for?

Goal

Learn what Intune admins expect from agentic experiences — including specifics about configuration, deployment, and monitoring workflows; permissions; audit requirements; and trust.

Approach

Generative interviews, plus a structured rating

19 interviews with Intune admins and Solutions Architects, including a rating exercise using the Cloud Security Alliance Autonomy Level Taxonomy.

Key insights
  • Risk is the deciding factor. Agents can't perform any task with a wide "blast radius," or one that could result in data loss or device unavailability.
  • Admins can't adopt agentic workflows until they've been successfully piloted, as with any other technology rollout.
  • Agents can't inherit the permissions of their human "pilots." They must use service accounts or their own credentials to satisfy audit requirements and zero-trust principles.
  • Humans retain control over scope. Agents must explicitly obtain permission from the human in the loop to access certain resources or take certain actions — and those permissions must be re-granted periodically.
  • In some cases, deterministic automations are more appropriate than agentic workflows.
  • Agents must abide by the same change-control, governance, and ITIL processes as human beings.
Impact

Foundational research that helped set the direction for agentic experiences. The article was referenced by the entire design team and the Agents core PM group, and the work was cited by fellow researchers.

My role. Research lead, end to end.

Case Study · Microsoft Intune · 2024

Intune Devices Workload JTBD Study

My partners kept coming to me for answers about admins' top tasks in the portal's most-visited surface. So I ran a large-scale JTBD study once — and turned it into durable, self-serve reference.

My role

Research lead — identified the need, ran it proactively

Methods

Survey (N=57) · JTBD interviews · job mapping

Participants

14 interviews — FTEs & Solutions Architects

Context

Answer it once, share it forever

My design and PM partners repeatedly came to me for answers about Intune admins' top tasks and workflows in the Devices workload — the most-visited surface in the portal. I proactively ran a large-scale JTBD study so I could share durable insights easily and quickly.

Goal

Capture Intune users' top tasks, job steps, pain points, and opportunities using the JTBD framework — functional, social, emotional, and related jobs; job performers; and success criteria. Then create a job map and quick-reference guide to inform stakeholders and enable self-service.

Approach

Survey to segment, interviews to deepen

A survey to capture jobs and segment them by job performer, plus 14 interviews with FTEs and Solutions Architects.

Key insights
  • Long lists, overlong journeys, duplicated entry points, non-human-readable errors and labels, ambiguous strings, and non-binary settings all impede task success and frustrate users.
  • Troubleshooting, monitoring, and reporting are phases in a cycle — and related jobs.
  • The job of configuring and deploying a policy starts and ends outside Microsoft tooling: users gather requirements, research documentation, translate stakeholder intent into Intune settings, and perform complex reporting in third-party tools like Power BI.
Impact

Foundational research that saved my stakeholders countless hours — and my most-referenced article in the Microsoft Research repository.

My role. Research lead — I identified the need and proactively initiated the project to meet it.
